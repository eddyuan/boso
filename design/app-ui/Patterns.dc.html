Layout patterns
The five shapes every screen in the app takes. Drawn as blocks rather than copy, so the structure is the subject and no language is implied.
List
Title, optional filters, then a stack of cards. Cards are the unit; the screen never mixes card widths.
Detail
Back row, subject, body, media, then actions along the bottom of the content. Reached by pushing, always dismissable by back.
Sheet over context
A peek at something without leaving where you are. Grab handle, one subject, at most two actions. Tapping outside dismisses.
Map with overlays
The map owns the whole screen. Everything else floats: chips at the top, a line and actions at the bottom, the tab bar clear of both.
Single-purpose form
One question per screen during onboarding, with the action pinned to the bottom. Never two decisions in one step.
Rules that hold across all five
✓
16px side padding, always. The only exception is the map, which bleeds.
✓
Scrolling screens clear the floating tab bar by its own content inset, since the bar overlaps content rather than sitting below it.
✓
One primary action per screen. If two things look equally important, neither is.
✓
An absent card is the empty state. A card that renders with nothing in it is worse than no card.
Text that has to stretch
Every label in these layouts is translated at runtime, so none of them can be sized to fit English. German and Finnish run roughly 30–40% longer; Japanese and Chinese run shorter but wrap differently.
✓
Rows grow vertically rather than truncating. Truncation is for names and places, not for instructions.
✓
Buttons size to content with a minimum, never a fixed width.
×
No two-line label squeezed into a one-line slot — it will be three lines somewhere.
×
No sentence built by concatenating fragments. Word order differs by language; a whole sentence per key.
×
No sentence wrapped around a link or a bold word in the layout — that fixes English word order in place. Named slots, filled by the translation.
Language is not region
Two independent settings, and conflating them is the easy mistake. Words follow the person; numbers, dates, distances and units follow the device. Somebody reading Chinese on a phone set to the US wants Chinese words and miles.
✓
“Match my device” is a real option in the picker, not a synonym for the default language.
✓
Each language named in itself — 简体中文, not “Chinese”.
×
Never a unit, separator or date order baked into a label.
The display face doesn’t cover every script
The rounded display and body faces are Latin-only. Left alone the platform substitutes per glyph, so one CJK sentence containing a name or a number arrives in two typefaces at two apparent weights.
✓
Hand the whole run to the system face for a script the fonts can’t draw, and restore weight explicitly.
✓
Sizes and line heights stay put; only the family changes.
×
No uppercasing or letter-spacing on a script that has neither case nor a need for it.
